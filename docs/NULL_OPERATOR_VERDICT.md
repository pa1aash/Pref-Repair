## 1C — The concavity mechanism, derived and checked against the code

`src/payoff.py` computes `payoff(x_t) = U_exo(x_t)/U_exo(x*_t)` with `U_exo(z) = z_A^0.5 z_B^0.5`
and `x*_t` the closed-form Cobb-Douglas optimum at `0.5/0.5` weights. Writing `s_t` for the
expenditure share on good A and substituting the budget-exhaustion identity `x_{t,A} = s_t I_t /
p_{t,A}`, `x_{t,B} = (1-s_t) I_t / p_{t,B}` (enforced everywhere in this project — see
`src/projection.py` constraint (5) and `src/budget_sets.py`'s prompts) into the code's actual
formula gives, after the price and income terms cancel exactly:

```
payoff(x_t) = 2 * sqrt( s_t * (1 - s_t) )
```

**This holds exactly** — confirmed by direct algebraic reduction of `payoff.py`'s literal code, not
assumed from `MAIN_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL.md`'s prose. It is strictly concave on `[0,1]`, symmetric
(`payoff(s) = payoff(1-s)`), with a single interior maximum `payoff(0.5) = 1` and
`payoff -> 0` as `s -> {0, 1}`. It depends on `x_t` **only** through `s_t` — not on price level, not
on income, not on which good is labeled A. Because `d(payoff)/ds = (1-2s)/sqrt(s(1-s))` is positive
for `s < 0.5` and negative for `s > 0.5`, **any movement of `s_t` toward `0.5`, from either side,
raises that observation's payoff monotonically, regardless of the mechanism that produced the
movement.** The Fable review's Jensen's-inequality argument is not an approximation or a
worst-case scenario here — it is an exact, unconditional property of the implemented payoff
function.

## 1D — The verdict

**Outcome 3 applies, and the evidence is stronger against C1 than Outcome 3's own description
anticipates.** Outcome 3 as specified requires "no significant difference between
`delta_payoff_real` and `delta_payoff_null`, and/or the partial correlation controlling for the
null effect is not significant." What was actually found is not merely "no difference" — it is a
**significant difference in the direction opposite to what C1 needs**:

```
mean delta_payoff_real = 0.0091     mean delta_payoff_null = 0.0220
Wilcoxon signed-rank p = 3.98e-10     paired t-test p = 0.00084
null > real on 69/85 traces (81%)
```

The size-matched, GARP-blind, center-pull null does not merely reproduce the real projection's
payoff gain — **it more than doubles it**, using the identical total L1 displacement budget the
real GARP-restoring projection used, at both models independently (qwen 1.5B: `p = 5.5e-08`;
llama 3B: `p = 0.0010`), not driven by a subset of traces or one arm of the design. If
GARP-restoration were buying payoff through some mechanism beyond the concavity/Jensen effect, it
should be at least as efficient at capturing that effect as an operator built to capture nothing
else; instead it is markedly *less* efficient, consistent with `docs/PAYOFF_AUDIT.md` §3's own
earlier finding that the real projection's movement is only weakly aligned (cosine 0.16-0.31) with
the direction toward the exogenous optimum — most of its displacement budget is spent on GARP's
ordering constraints, not on the direction that this payoff function actually rewards.

**The one complication, stated honestly rather than smoothed over:** the partial correlation of
dose with `delta_payoff_real`, controlling for `delta_payoff_null`, remains significant
(`r = 0.574`, `t = 6.35`, `p = 1.13e-08`) — attenuated from the unconditional `r = 0.821`
(`MAIN_EXPERIMENT_RESULTS.md` §4's own figure) but not eliminated. This does **not** rescue C1's
specific claim. C1's claim under test is that the *GARP-specific direction* of repair buys payoff
beyond what *bare displacement magnitude toward the center* would buy on its own — and the paired
comparison directly contradicts that: bare center-directed displacement buys *more*, not less, at
matched magnitude. The surviving partial correlation is better read as evidence that dose (however
it arises) tracks the concavity mechanism through a channel this particular null does not fully
capture — plausibly because `delta_payoff_null` is a within-trace deterministic function of `dose`
and `d_center` alone (`rho(dose, null) = 0.912`), while `delta_payoff_real` also depends on exactly
how the GARP-constrained solver happened to allocate its budget across the 25 periods, which
introduces trace-specific noise the null's simpler formula does not model. That is measurement
detail about *how well this specific null captures the mechanism*, not evidence that GARP-restoration
has an effect independent of the mechanism.

**Conclusion: C1 as currently stated in `docs/FRAMING.md` — "the GARP-specific projection does
something beyond what geometry alone would produce at the same displacement magnitude" — is not
supported by this control. The evidence points the other way: geometry alone, spent efficiently,
outperforms the GARP-specific projection at the same budget.** This is not a borderline or
inconclusive call; both the paired-magnitude test and its per-model split are significant at
`p < 0.005` in every cut, in the direction against C1.

**Per the operator's brief: this requires an operator decision before any further paper work
happens.** The dose-response relationship documented in `MAIN_EXPERIMENT_RESULTS.md` §4 is real and
precisely estimated, but this control indicates it is substantially explained by the payoff
function's own concavity rather than by GARP-restoration specifically — the paper's contribution
likely needs to pivot toward what survives this control, most plausibly `docs/FRAMING.md`'s C2
(capacity-confound framing) and/or C3 (the discard-selection finding), neither of which this
control touches, rather than resting on C1 as currently written. **This session does not attempt
that pivot** — deciding what the paper's headline claim becomes, one day before the deadline, is a
scope decision for the operator, not a documentation-session writing task.

**Track 3 (the paper-integration session) should not proceed to weave C1 into the paper as
currently stated until the operator has made this call.** If the decision is to keep a dose-response
claim in some form, it must be rewritten to state explicitly that the relationship is largely a
property of the exogenous payoff's own geometry rather than of GARP-restoration per se, with these
numbers (not the raw `rho = 0.729`) as the honest headline; if the decision is to pivot the
contribution toward C2/C3, Track 3's scope changes substantially and should be re-briefed by the
operator rather than inferred.

## What would change this call

Nothing about this result looks fragile or artifact-driven: the reconstruction cross-check matched
the existing pipeline's own numbers to `1e-6` on every trace, `lambda` was never clipped (the
matching is exact, not approximate, for all 85 traces), and the paired result replicates
independently at both models with `p < 0.005` in the weaker (3B) arm alone. The one thing that
could legitimately move this verdict is a different, independently-motivated null operator
construction — e.g., one that also respects some notion of "plausible in this task" beyond pure
budget-line geometry — if the operator judges the direct-to-center null tested here to be an
unfair comparison. `docs/NULL_OPERATOR_METHOD.md` §3 argues explicitly for why direct-to-center is
the correct operationalization of the reviewer's own stated mechanism, not a strawman, but that is
a judgment call worth the operator's own eyes given how much rides on it.
