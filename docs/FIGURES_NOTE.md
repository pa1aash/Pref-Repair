# Figure design notes (Part Three)

Three figures, generated by `scripts/make_figures.py` from `results/main_ccei.json` and the
pilot/main numbers transcribed (and cited inline in the script) from `docs/PILOT_RESULTS.md` and
`docs/MAIN_EXPERIMENT_RESULTS.md`. No numbers are invented in the script; every plotted value
traces to one of those two files or to a computation over `main_ccei.json` identical to
`src/analyse_main.py`'s own.

**Palette.** dataviz skill's validated categorical slots 1/2/3 (blue `#2a78d6`, orange `#eb6834`,
aqua unused). Color is assigned by entity and held fixed across all three figures: **1.5B
(headroom model) is always blue, 3B (null-control model) is always orange.** Figure 2 breaks this
convention deliberately — its entity is protocol version, not model, so blue marks the
retry-corrected (trustworthy) main-experiment numbers and a desaturated grey marks the pilot's
naive, survivorship-biased numbers, so the "which number should you trust" signal is carried by
color as well as position.

**Titles are descriptive, not argumentative.** In-image titles/suptitles state what is plotted
(axes, comparison, sample sizes) rather than the conclusion drawn from it — e.g. fig2's title is
"1.5B reciprocal-framing CCEI comparison: pilot vs. main experiment," not "the effect vanishes."
The interpretive claim belongs in the paper's `\caption{}` text (Part Four), not baked into the
figure itself, so the figure remains reusable if the interpretation is later qualified.

**Axis-spacing check.** Figure 1's two panels originally had independently-scaled x-axes (1.5B's
doses ran to ~112, 3B's to ~56); this was corrected to a **shared x-range across both panels**
specifically so that the visual steepness of the two trend lines is directly comparable — an
unshared axis would have let the 3B panel's shorter x-range visually flatten or steepen its slope
relative to 1.5B's independent of the actual data. Figure 2 and 3 use single, appropriately-scaled
axes per panel; none use a dual y-axis.

## Cold-read verdicts (no caption present)

**Figure 1 (dose–response, both models).** Mostly survives. Axis labels state exactly what is
plotted (projection dose vs. Δ exogenous payoff), each panel is titled by model, and the
Spearman ρ/p/n annotations let a reader recover the statistical claim (positive, significant,
both models; steeper/tighter at 1.5B) without reading prose. What does **not** survive
cold: a reader unfamiliar with the project would not know what "exogenous payoff" means or why
3B is called a "null control" — that context is necessarily supplied by the caption and method
section, not recoverable from the plot alone. Acceptable: this is normal for a quantity defined
earlier in the paper, not a figure-design flaw.

**Figure 2 (discard-selection bias, pilot vs. main).** Partially survives. Each panel is legible
on its own (top: a CCEI delta flips sign and shrinks toward zero across two labeled x-groups;
bottom: a discard rate falls 52%→43%→20% across three labeled x-groups), but the **causal link
between the two panels** — that the top panel's change happened *because* of the bottom panel's
correction — is not itself drawn on the page. A reader would recover "these numbers changed" and
"discard rates fell" as two separate facts, not necessarily their relationship, without a caption
sentence connecting them. This is the figure most dependent on its caption; Part Four's caption
should say so explicitly in one sentence (e.g., "top: as the residual discard rate below falls
from 52% to 20%, the naive framing 'effect' collapses toward zero and changes sign").

**Figure 3 (multiturn/format effect, 1.5B).** Survives well. The two-panel bar contrast is the
strongest of the three read cold: GARP pass rate collapses (40%→10%, labeled, p annotated) while
mean CCEI is visually flat with heavily overlapping error bars (p annotated) — the "GARP pass rate
is the sensitive instrument, CCEI is not" claim is legible directly from the two panels' shapes
without needing the surrounding prose. The zero-discard fact is stated in the suptitle rather than
shown as data (there is no third panel for it, since both bars would be identically zero and add
no visual information) — acceptable, but the caption should still state it as a sentence since it
is the reason this comparison is cleaner than Figures 1 or 2's reciprocal-framing arm.
