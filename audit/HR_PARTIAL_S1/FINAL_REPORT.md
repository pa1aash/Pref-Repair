# Repairing Choice Coherence in AI Agents: What Has Been Built, What It Bought, and What Remains Unmeasured

**The first half of the question is occupied, and not marginally.** A substantial set of published and preprinted systems does not merely score an AI agent's choice consistency but actively changes it. At inference time: a quadratic program projecting a model's stated event probabilities onto the coherent simplex — raw atomic probabilities in one forecasting task summed to 2.121 — paired with an iterative max-di-cut routine approximating a Kemeny ranking (the ranking minimising total pairwise disagreement) to break preference cycles [1]; and likelihood-aware aggregation that cuts a judging model's pairwise transitivity inconsistency more than threefold without retraining [2]. At training time: an isotonic projection of a reward model's pointwise scores onto the monotone cone induced by its own pairwise labels [3]; a dual-track framework enforcing transitivity, symmetry and functional uniqueness on extracted relations [4]; rejection sampling onto the region of choice space satisfying the Generalized Axiom of Revealed Preference (GARP — no revealed-preference cycle containing a strict relation), used to build a synthetic fine-tuning corpus that cuts out-of-sample bundle-prediction error [5]; and a fine-tuning objective whose reflexivity, independence-of-irrelevant-alternatives and cycle-additivity terms are literally axiom penalties [6]. Two further constructions are fully specified and unrun: a training penalty equal to one minus the Critical Cost Efficiency Index [7], and a reward-aggregation rule that provably restores Condorcet consistency under arbitrary cyclic preference profiles [8].

**The second half — whether enforced coherence changes downstream decision quality — has also been tested, repeatedly, in both directions, and every published estimate is unidentified.** Reported signs disagree not because the effect is subtle but because the comparisons are confounded. Where a scalar operator projects onto the same set its outcome metric grades, gains are near-automatic and near-tautological — but where the graded object is a *vector* of axioms, enforcing one consumes another [6]. Where operator and metric come apart, three independent attempts across three different axiom systems moved the graded outcome the wrong way: a choice-revision arm that lowered mean Afriat efficiency by 0.025 [11], a monotone recalibration that raised the conditional-independence violation it was meant to remove in fourteen of sixteen model-by-domain cells [9], and an axiom-constrained latent model whose held-out squared error rose from 0.0927 to 0.1014 even as its incoherence fell by a factor of 3.5 [10]. On the other side, the two peer-reviewed ablations usually cited for the proposition that *relaxing* coherence helps compare a strictly richer model class against a poorer one, varying representational capacity and coherence together by construction [12][13].

**The most consequential fact in the census is not the count but the shape.** Every implemented operator acts on a preference proxy — a judging model's rankings, an annotator pool's comparisons, a reward head's scores, a training corpus — or on a probability vector. Exactly one system targets GARP by name, and it does so *generatively*: it rejection-samples a consistent synthetic corpus before training rather than projecting an observed choice sequence [5]. Nothing in the corpus takes an agent's own realised choices over budget sets, perturbs them minimally onto the rationalizable set, and scores the result against a payoff that no preference judgment enters. That conjunction — own choices, cardinal dose, exogenous outcome — is the narrow cell the surrounding density makes visible.

The two fronts work on the same object with different vocabularies and almost no shared citations, and the cost of that is measurable.

## 1. What Counts as an Intervention, and What Counts as Mere Measurement

### A. Functionals, operators, and three grades of intervention

The distinction the question turns on is not theory versus practice, nor economics versus machine learning, nor inference-time versus training-time. It is the distinction between a **functional** — a map from a dataset to a number, reporting how badly the data violate a condition and changing nothing — and an **operator**, a map from a dataset to another dataset, returning modified choices, scores, probabilities or weights such that the condition holds or holds more nearly. The revealed-preference literature is unusually careful about this boundary, naming both objects: a *goodness-of-fit index* on one side, a *loss function* whose argument-minimum is a repaired object on the other [16].

Three grades separate cleanly, and works are routinely miscategorised across them.

**Grade I — diagnostic index.** Afriat's Critical Cost Efficiency Index (CCEI: the largest budget-shrinkage factor at which the data satisfy relaxed GARP), the Houtman–Maks index (the largest fraction of observations that are jointly rationalizable), the Varian index and the money-pump index all live here. They quantify; they do not repair. GARP itself, in the form Afriat's theorem gives it (§2A), is a binary test, and the indices exist precisely because a binary test carries little information about severity [14].

**Grade II — post-hoc repair operator.** The operator takes the realised output and returns a nearby output satisfying the condition. Economics has fully specified members of this class: mixed-integer linear programs (MILPs: optimisations over both continuous and binary variables) finding the minimum average expenditure adjustment, or the minimum price perturbation, that restores GARP [15]. Machine learning has independently built its own: a weighted least-squares quadratic program returning the coherent probability distribution closest to a model's stated probabilities [1]; the Pool-Adjacent-Violators Algorithm — the standard solver for isotonic, i.e. order-preserving, regression — applied to a reward model's pointwise scores under the partial order its own pairwise labels induce [3]; likelihood-weighted tie-breaking that resolves judge cycles as they are detected [2]; and constraint-based verification with targeted re-prompting on the specific violated triples [4].

**Grade III — installed constraint.** The condition is imposed before the output exists: as a penalty in the loss [7], a reweighting of the objective so that its maximum-likelihood solution satisfies named social-choice axioms [8], or a filter on the training distribution [5]. The query's four verbs map onto these grades: CORRECTS and REPAIRS are Grade II; PROJECTS is the strict subset of Grade II that performs a minimum-distance projection onto a consistency-defined set, tracked in §6's *Formally minimal?* column; ENFORCES is Grade III, plus the generative case.

### B. Three boundary cases that inflate the count

- **Abstention is not repair.** Running the same agent repeatedly and answering only when three runs agree lifts accuracy to 87–88% at 54–62% coverage, a 6-to-14-point gain over single-run baselines — but the agent's choices are not thereby made more consistent; the inconsistent cases are removed from the sample [17].
- **Level-steering is not projection.** Activation steering along a control vector — a difference direction computed from contrasting-pair prompts and added to the residual stream — reliably shifts allocations toward payoff maximisation [18], and a steering vector built by aligning a behaviourally elicited risk-preference map to residual-stream activations shifts risky-choice probabilities [19]; both move the *location* of a preference, not the *coherence* of a choice sequence.
- **Architectural augmentation is not enforcement.** Surveys catalogue tools, symbolic reasoners, explicit utility functions and conformal risk controls as routes to "more rational" agents [20]; these change an agent's inputs or computation, not the algebraic structure of its choices.

One structural fact organises everything downstream: the set an operator projects onto and the set a metric grades need not be the same set, and when they diverge the intervention can execute perfectly and still make the graded quantity worse.

## 2. The Economics Front: Afriat, Varian, Houtman-Maks and What They Offer as Repair

### A. The characterisation layer

Afriat's theorem (1967) is the hinge: a finite dataset of price–quantity pairs is rationalizable by a continuous, monotone, concave utility function if and only if it satisfies GARP, equivalently if and only if the finite system of Afriat inequalities is feasible [14][7]. A bundle chosen at one date is *directly revealed preferred* to anything affordable at that date's prices, strictly so if the alternative was strictly cheaper; GARP forbids the transitive closure of the weak relation from contradicting any strict direct relation. The Weak Axiom (WARP), Samuelson's 1938 original, is the two-observation restriction — if one bundle is chosen when a second is affordable, the second is never chosen when the first is affordable — and the Strong Axiom (SARP), Houthakker's 1950 closure of it, additionally forbids indifference cycles, so it is incompatible with genuine indifference [14]. GARP sits between them and is the version Afriat's theorem characterises. The productive research programme has been finding *modified* versions of GARP characterising rationalization by utility functions with extra structure: concave, homothetic, quasi-linear, single-peaked [14]. This front is therefore not one axiom but a parameterised family, each member carrying its own repair problem.

### B. The severity layer and its complexity stratification

The apparatus grew from a specific irritation with Afriat's equivalence: it is all-or-nothing. Varian's 1985 paper is the founding move, framed explicitly against the binary character of the existing tests — the data either satisfy the optimisation hypothesis or they do not, with no channel for measurement error — and proposing instead to derive a test statistic from *the size of the minimal perturbation needed to restore consistency* [21]. Every later minimum-perturbation index descends from that sentence.

Five indices now measure how badly a dataset fails.

- **CCEI** deflates budgets by a common factor and reports the largest factor at which cycles vanish; it is computable in polynomial time by binary search with a combinatorial GARP check at each step [7].
- **The Varian index** refines this to observation-specific deflations.
- **The Houtman–Maks index** deletes whole observations.
- **The Dean–Martin minimum cost index** deletes revealed-preference *relations*, weighted by money-metric cost.
- **The money-pump index** measures the dollars an arbitrageur could extract by trading against the agent's own revealed choices, as a share of expenditure [22].

Two properties transfer directly to artificial agents. First, **violations are common but shallow.** In a scanner panel of 494 households, 396 — about 80% — violate GARP at some point, yet the mean money-pump index is roughly 6% of food expenditure, about $12.80 against an average monthly outlay of $213, and the formal test cannot reject rationality once measurement error is allowed [22]. Shallow violations are precisely the regime in which minimal repair is cheap, and the same shallowness now appears in language models: across twelve models tested on binary menus, **transitivity ranges from 0.957 to 1.000** [6].

Second, **the severity layer is computationally stratified in a way that maps onto repair difficulty.** Detecting a violation is easy: a strongly-connected-component decomposition of the revealed-preference graph yields an index computable in O(n²) time [23]. Deleting relations at minimum cost is NP-hard, by reduction from maximum acyclic subgraph [23]. The Houtman–Maks and average Varian indices are likewise NP-hard [15] — but that claim carries a commodity count that is almost always dropped, and dropping it inverts it in the operative regime. The Houtman–Maks deletion problem is **polynomial-time solvable at two goods**, by reduction to vertex cover on perfect graphs, and NP-complete only from three [66]; two goods is exactly the regime every budget-set study of a language model runs. In general the deletion problem is approximation-equivalent to directed feedback vertex set, and neither Houtman–Maks nor the average Varian index admits a constant-factor polynomial-time approximation unless P = NP [15].

None of this is fatal in practice. MILP formulations replacing Afriat's cardinal utility-and-multiplier pair with ordinal levels in the unit interval and binary comparison indicators keep every constraint linear jointly in utilities, prices and quantities, and they solve all three hard indices in under two seconds on real subject-level panels [15]. Those timings belong to *measurement* — indices computed over fixed data with a combinatorial or scalar-bisection oracle — and do not transfer to a program in which the bundles are decision variables and the revealed-preference relation is therefore endogenous; the least-squares version of that program is a mixed-integer *quadratic* program rather than a MILP. The operating point for an agent experiment is small, but it is not the one these timings measure.

### C. The repair layer, and its one missing cell

This is where the economics front is strongest and least known outside it. The same machinery yields explicit *perturbation* operators, not just indices. The Average Expenditure Error introduces non-negative slacks on each observation's expenditure, relaxes the revealed-preference relation accordingly, and minimises mean slack subject to relaxed GARP — fully specified, with inequalities [15]. The Average Price Error introduces perturbed price vectors, requires GARP to hold at the perturbed prices with observed quantities, and minimises the mean distance between observed and perturbed prices — fully specified, with a corollary and five inequality blocks [15]. The parallel object for perturbing *quantities* — the agent's actual choices — is named in two sentences, with no inequalities, no program, no complexity classification, and no mention in the paper's conclusion: introduce variables q̃ₜ, define GARP consistency conditional on those bundles, and minimise the mean of ‖qₜ − q̃ₜ‖ subject to (pₜ, q̃ₜ) satisfying GARP [15]. **The economics front has a worked minimal-perturbation repair for prices and for expenditures, and only a sketch for the object an agent experiment would want to perturb: the agent's own chosen bundles.**

The sketch is fillable rather than merely empty, and the same paper says why: its Theorem 2 drops Afriat's multipliers for ordinal levels in the unit interval plus binary comparison indicators, leaving every inequality linear jointly in utilities, prices **and quantities**, so promoting the bundle to a decision variable keeps the program mixed-integer *linear* — whereas the Afriat parameterisation turns bilinear the moment the bundle is free and stays bilinear even with the ordering fixed [15]. One heuristic for the quantity problem is also published, and it is the prior work a reader in this literature asks about first: De Peretti's iterative procedure tackles violations one at a time, perturbs one observation at a time, and takes the cheaper of a cycle's two orientations, with no optimality guarantee and no complexity claim attached [62].

That object is named elsewhere, and the naming matters. The non-linear **Least Squares index** is defined as the distance between the observed data and the nearest GARP-satisfying dataset — minimal perturbation onto the rationalizable set, a standing index in this literature rather than a new coinage [16]. The same source explains why the operator is delicate. **The set of GARP-obeying datasets is not closed**: one can construct a sequence of GARP-satisfying two-observation datasets, budget constraints held fixed, whose limit violates GARP. It follows that no goodness-of-fit index can be simultaneously continuous and accurate — the Afriat, Varian, swaps and least-squares indices are continuous but inaccurate, Houtman–Maks is accurate but discontinuous [16]. Worse for an operator: for the Varian, swaps and least-squares losses, if the data violate GARP then the set of minimising well-behaved utility functions is *empty*, so the infimum is approached but not attained [16]. And "inaccurate" has a sharper operational meaning than the word suggests — the least-squares distance can read exactly **0** on a dataset that violates GARP, because that dataset sits at a limit point of GARP-obeying ones, a *cusp dataset*. An operator built by minimising it can therefore report "no repair needed" on data that needs repair, silently [16]. Anyone reporting "the" minimal repair of a choice sequence on the unrestricted utility class is reporting an arbitrary selection.

Two escapes are known, both working by restoring closedness on a subset: restrict to homothetic preferences or to expected utility with a concave Bernoulli function [16]; or restrict to a parametric family and decompose goodness-of-fit into an *inconsistency* component and a *misspecification* component [24]. Imposing a fixed strict-preference margin γ does the same job operationally, but it buys attainment by shrinking the target: what such a program computes is the **γ-margin projection**, onto a closed *subset* of the GARP-consistent set, so the reported distance is a γ-indexed upper bound on the least-squares index rather than the index itself. That converts an unattained infimum into an attained minimum and a silent failure into a reported parameter — one that has to be reported and swept, not passed off as an implementation detail.

### D. Minimal perturbation beyond GARP

Two further constructions belong in any honest census. For any positive distance, one can characterise exactly which choice-under-risk datasets admit a rationalization within that distance — in beliefs, in state-utility weights, or in perceived prices — of expected-utility theory, with the three perturbation channels shown equivalent [25]. This is minimal-perturbation restoration of rationalizability in the strict sense the question asks about, for the subjective-expected-utility axiom system rather than GARP; its limitation is that the index depends on the data only through the ordering of payoffs and is therefore piecewise constant [7]. Separately, via optimal transport, the maximum money-pump profit extractable from a consumer equals both the minimum overpayment needed to attain his utility targets and the minimum wasted quasilinear utility, each minimised over all candidate utility functions [26]. That construction recovers a utility function even from non-optimal choices, and the recovered function belongs to a fully rational consumer who systematically *mis-perceives prices* — structural repair of a different kind, and an apt reading for a language model, where prompt framing is precisely a mechanism by which perceived constraints could be distorted.

Modern extensions continue in the same diagnostic register: an Afriat theorem for single-peaked utilities that rejects adapting CCEI, because corner-anchored budgets provide no natural deflation point, and falls back on a mixed-integer Houtman–Maks computation [27]; non-parametric heterogeneity methods for scanner data [28]; and an export beyond humans, using Afriat's theorem to detect constrained utility maximisation from the observed actions of engineering systems [29] — measurement, not repair, but proof that nothing here requires the agent to be human.

Economics, then, has implemented repair operators, classified their complexity, identified their well-posedness failure mode and named two escapes. What it has not done is *work out* the continuous quantity-perturbation program — the formulation is sketched and one non-optimal heuristic exists, but no exact, budget-constrained, complexity-classified version does — or point any of the apparatus at an artificial agent as an operator rather than as a score. The reliability of the indices themselves is contested; §7F weighs that evidence.

## 3. The Economics Front Applied to AI Agents: Who Has Intervened

### A. The measurement wave, which is the contrast class

Enumerating the measurement wave keeps the intervention count honest.

| Design | What it reports |
|---|---|
| Budget-set GARP testing across risk, time, social and food domains [30][7] | Efficiency scores above human benchmarks — CCEI averages above 0.997 — robust to sampling randomness and demographic framing but sensitive to the linguistic framing of the choice situation |
| A taxonomy of fine-grained rationality elements scored across fourteen models [31] | Size affects the ability to behave rationally |
| Nearly forty models put through 161 rounds of priced moral dilemmas against a null distribution of random choosers [32] | Two passes at the 1% level, five more at 5% and two more at 10%, with pairwise co-classification into the same rationalizable type running 24% to 48% |
| Prompt-based role specialisation [33] | *Increases* axiom violations and lowers the efficiency index |
| Holding a model fixed and re-running a 25-round budget task 100 times [34] | A full CCEI distribution rather than a point |
| A central-bank working paper testing whether a reservation-wage policy exists in a sequential search environment [18] | Larger models rationalizable nearly always, the smallest (Mistral v0.3 7B) not rationalizable at all, and reasoning-tuned variants *worse* than their base, with recovered discount factors clustering between 0.2 and 0.8 |
| A discrete-choice model recovering the cost function that best rationalizes a model's choices across four clinical diagnosis domains [35] | Revealed-preference estimates explain realised decisions far better than verbalised cost ratios |
| A companion test of whether elicited beliefs are decision-sufficient [9] | They are not: conditional mutual information is significantly positive in all sixteen cells |

**None of these indices is interpretable without the power of the design that produced it, and the power runs opposite to the usual worry.** The two-good, 25-budget design behind the headline efficiency scores carries **99.9% Bronars power** against uniform-random choosers, independently replicated at 100.0% with a mean simulated efficiency index of 0.718: two goods is not a limitation of that design, it is the reason the design works. Power falls exponentially in the number of **goods**, not from having only two — under Selten's area measure the empirical content of GARP converges to zero at rate exp(−cK), reaching an area of 0.9996 at 24 goods and 10 observations, and the known remedies slow that loss without eliminating it [63] — so any move toward a realistic action space buys realism by spending test content.

What is genuinely weak here is *conditional* power: bootstrapping the model's own choices gives a 7.9–26.3% probability of rejecting GARP against 95.4–99.8% for humans, which indicts the model's preference homogeneity rather than the budget sets. The general discipline follows from the identity predictive success = pass rate − (1 − power): a 0.957 GARP pass rate obtained at 8.8% power is worth 0.045, and on one published altruism design 31% of uniform-random agents clear a CCEI of 0.99 outright. Power depends only on prices and budgets, never on the agent's choices, so it is computable before a single query is spent and there is no excuse for reporting an index without it.

None of these changes anything. That is what makes them the contrast class.

### B. The four crossings

**Generative enforcement.** A transformer-based probabilistic time-series foundation model is fine-tuned by low-rank adaptation (LoRA: training small rank-constrained weight updates instead of the full model; rank 16, 5,000 steps) on 50,000 synthetic agent panels generated by rejection sampling — draw Dirichlet budget shares, place the bundle on the budget hyperplane, accept only if adding it creates no GARP violation under a depth-first search over the revealed-preference graph [5]. Afriat's equivalence licenses this: sampling on GARP is sampling from a very large class of utility maximisers without ever writing a utility function down. On a real portfolio-choice panel of 154 consumers over 50 budget scenarios, **out-of-sample bundle prediction error falls 17–18%** at forecast horizons 5, 10 and 15 and 31% at horizon 1, with a random-feasible-budget control at roughly double the error confirming the model exploits real structure [5].

Two details matter more than the headline. Only 20 of 154 real consumers satisfy GARP exactly, yet the coherence-instilled model improves prediction for 121 of them and *worsens* it for 33. And the correlation between an individual's efficiency index and the gain they receive is 0.456, with exact passers at mean bundle fitness 0.723 against 0.688 for the rest — positive, but by the authors' own statement not high enough to make CCEI a reliable screening rule [5].

**Weight-level axiom enforcement.** Llama 3.1 8B Instruct is fine-tuned toward two principal-specified risk-aversion targets with a multi-term loss whose auxiliary terms are, term by term, revealed-preference axioms:

- a **reflexivity** term penalising a non-zero logit gap on placebo menus where both labels describe the same portfolio;
- a **mirror** term penalising asymmetry under label-swapping;
- two **independence-of-irrelevant-alternatives** terms tying the average and dispersion of the three-option logit gap to the two-option gap for the same pair, so that adding a strictly dominated third alternative cannot shift the odds between the original two;
- a **cycle** term penalising the additivity residual across binary triads with a sign-margin hinge — transitivity written as a loss;
- and a **path** term forcing interpolation paths to bracket the indifference surface [6].

The effects are large and, crucially, *not monotone*. The near-risk-neutral target reaches reflexivity 0.9941, monotonicity 1.0000, transitivity 1.0000 and independence 0.9484 against a baseline of 0.920 — the highest in a twelve-model pool — with the structural parameter recovered at 0.000087 against a target of 0.0001. The more risk-averse target reaches independence 0.8000 and transitivity 0.9583, both *below* baseline [6]. A logit-scale sweep exposes the mechanism: independence falls from 0.948 at scale 1 to 0.857 at scale 2 to 0.496 at scale 4, because a larger utility gap makes the model more sensitive to the content of each option and hence more responsive to a dominated third alternative [6]. This is the cleanest demonstration available that WARP-family consistency in a language model is a trainable, dose-sensitive quantity rather than a fixed property. No capability benchmark is run afterwards, so what the intervention cost elsewhere is unknown.

**Inference-time steering.** Control vectors reliably shift allocation-game behaviour toward payoff maximisation, and prompt masking does the same less precisely; effectiveness is task-dependent, with one model steerable across a wide patience range in the sequential search environment while another does not respond at any coefficient. The authors' own summary is that as task complexity increases, preference coherence declines *and becomes harder to manipulate* [18]. Prompt-level steering is weaker still: given an explicit target cost function, only 14.6% to 31.2% of attempts land within 80–120% of the way to the target, and 12.5% to 27.1% move in the wrong direction outright [35]. Cost-function prompting also *lowers* behavioural consistency relative to supplying accurate probabilities — one model runs 74.6% at baseline, 99.2% given true probabilities and 100.0% given its own elicited probabilities, but only 77.3% under cost-function prompting [35].

**Label-free regularization, specified and unrun.** Three penalties, each continuous, each polynomial-time computable, each zero if and only if the corresponding representation theorem's axiom holds:

- the **Dutch-book magnitude**, obtained as the optimal value of a linear program bounded by total stake;
- **one minus CCEI**, via binary search with a GARP check at each step;
- and **one minus the subjective-expected-utility distance**, computable by binary search over the finitely many payoff ratios [7].

A supporting lemma addresses the known CCEI loophole, but only inside its hypothesis: with prices drawn independently from Lebesgue densities and budget-exhausting choices, CCEI equals 1 if and only if GARP holds, with probability one [7].

That is a guarantee about a *sampler*, not a property of the estimator, and it does not survive the design the LLM literature actually inherits, which quotes prices to two decimals — a 91-point discrete grid with no density. On integer prices at 25 observations every dataset contains at least one exact budget-equality tie; 18.9% of uniform-random agents then report an index of exactly 1.0 while genuinely violating GARP, against zero such events in 63,200 continuous-price replications carrying 12,102 real GARP failures, and a near-rational agent on a quarter-point grid still trips the false certificate 8.7% of the time. The proposal is explicitly for insertion into a training loss, with adversarial problem generation and a requirement that paraphrase-induced violations count. No model is trained in it [7].

### C. What this front has and has not done

The economics-side contribution is real and lopsided. Where the apparatus has been applied generatively — as a prior over training data — it produced the corpus's cleanest win on a metric no preference judgment enters [5]. Where it has been proposed as a projection or a loss penalty, it has not been run [7]. And the one group that measures rationalizability rigorously intervenes on preference *levels* rather than on coherence [18]. The post-hoc cell is thin not because nobody thought of enforcing GARP on an agent, but because the corresponding economics program exists only as a two-sentence sketch and one greedy, order-dependent heuristic [15][62], neither ever aimed at an artificial agent. A second asymmetry sits inside the apparatus itself: Afriat's index has been computed on agents repeatedly [30][33][34][18], while neither the Varian index nor Houtman–Maks has been computed on an artificial agent's choices in any work found. The AI-facing economics work reaches only for the index that is polynomial, leaving the two NP-hard ones unused even though integer programming solves both in under two seconds at agent-experiment scale [15].

## 4. The ML/Alignment Front: Coherence Theorems, Bradley-Terry Violations, and Nash Learning

The machine-learning front reached the same axioms through a different door, and split into two camps usually read as disagreeing about facts. They are not. They are operating on different objects.

### A. The coherence-theorem pole, and its published demolition

The normative motivation is a family of exploitability arguments. An agent with cyclic preferences can be walked around the cycle and returned to its starting point one penny poorer — a "money pump"; an agent whose probabilities do not sum to one can be Dutch-booked, sold a set of bets it evaluates as individually favourable that jointly guarantee a loss. The family converges on one conclusion: if you are not shooting yourself in the foot in sense X, you can be viewed as having coherence property Y [36].

The canonical exposition disclaims almost everything an enforcement programme would want from it. On rigour: nothing in it is a coherence theorem *per se*, only intuitive arguments that one ought to exist, and nothing a mathematician would consider a proof. On content: the theorem says nothing about which things to value more than others. On scope: valuing the emotional state of certainty may simply be a fact about what an agent values, even if it sits outside the expected-utility formalism [36]. The strongest available statement of the pro-coherence position declines to be a theorem, declines to constrain content, and concedes that a real preference can live outside the formalism.

The counter-case is peer-reviewed and already published. Global coherence is not rationally required, since completeness is not a rationality requirement and only coherent extendibility is, so a rational agent need not be representable as an expected-utility maximizer; coherent expected-utility maximization is generally intractable, so a bounded agent that insists on complying with the axioms must give up even approximate optimality with respect to its own objective; and coherence is nearly uninformative about which goals a system will pursue [37]. A parallel critique annotates 94 value-alignment papers and finds the majority never define values at all, relying on preferences as a stand-in that risks reducing culturally situated concepts to binary choices [38].

Two distinct failures are routinely conflated here. That coherence *implies* competence is settled in the negative as a matter of philosophy — a standing peer-reviewed thesis, not an available finding. That *enforcing* a given quantity of coherence moves an exogenous payoff by some amount, for a fixed agent, is a quantitative question no side of the philosophical dispute has measured. The second is not answered by the first.

### B. Bradley–Terry as an acyclicity constraint in disguise

The pivotal formal result concerns the Bradley–Terry model — the pairwise-comparison model underlying almost all reward modelling in reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), which sets the probability that response *i* beats *j* to a logistic function of a scalar score difference [40]. Preferences over generated responses admit a scalar reward representation **if and only if** the preference relation is free of Condorcet cycles, cyclic majority preferences of the form A beats B beats C beats A [39]. That is Afriat's condition restated over a menu of responses instead of a sequence of budget sets. And under the Luce model, a general probabilistic preference model of which Bradley–Terry is a special case, Condorcet cycles arise with probability converging to one *exponentially fast* as the annotator population grows [39]. Scalar-reward alignment is not merely an approximation at population scale; it asks for something that provably does not exist.

Three refinements matter. First, the Bradley–Terry maximum-likelihood estimate implicitly implements the **Borda count** — the reward ordering coincides with the ordering by summed pairwise win probability — and Borda violates majority, pairwise-majority and Condorcet consistency [8]. Second, Bradley–Terry is not *necessary*: a reward model need only preserve the correct ranking under a monotone transform, a property called order consistency, and a simple upper-bound algorithm compatible with off-the-shelf binary classifiers is an order-consistent alternative, evaluated across more than 12,000 experimental setups spanning six base models and two datasets [41].

Third, once the representation through which responses are scored is itself learned, the impossibility becomes a **measurable trade-off**. The excess cross-entropy loss of any embedding-based reward decomposes exactly into an embedding term that a richer representation shrinks and an agreement term that a richer representation enlarges, because refining the embedding exposes more distinguishable pairs and therefore more cycles; the agreement term is lower-bounded by a curvature-weighted Condorcet-cycle probability at the decision margin. On synthetic data and three real preference datasets, embedding loss falls and agreement cost rises with dimension, total excess loss is often minimised at an *intermediate* dimension, and jointly training the embedding and the reward head provably does not reliably find that point [42]. This is the most precise statement in the corpus of what enforcing rationalizability costs: representational fidelity, at an exchange rate that rises with the sharpness of the instrument.

### C. The Nash pole

The alternative response to intransitivity is to change the solution concept rather than the data. Nash learning from human feedback replaces the reward model with a *pairwise preference model* and defines the objective as the Nash equilibrium of a symmetric two-player constant-sum preference game — the "von Neumann winner", a mixed strategy over responses that remains well defined when the preference relation is cyclic [43]. The original paper is explicit that its departure from scalar rewards is motivated by transitivity failures, including a worked example in which every individual is transitive yet the population-averaged pairwise preference is not, and it introduces a mirror-descent method whose last iterate provably converges to the regularized equilibrium [43].

Descendants extend the idea rather than the enforcement:

- a proximal-point outer loop removes a stability restriction on the regularization coefficient and yields last-iterate guarantees without it [44];
- a multiplayer generalisation replaces the single opponent with a population [45];
- an exploratory variant achieves O(√T) regret without exponential dependence on the KL-regularization parameter, improvable to O(log T) with a minimax oracle [46];
- and a social-choice framing shows the Nash family approximates **maximal lotteries** — the randomised social-choice rule that is majority-undominated — inheriting majority support and robustness to irrelevant alternatives [47], with a leaderboard-aggregation application that assumes nothing about preference structure [48].

The statistical companion identifies when the equilibrium stays genuinely mixed rather than collapsing to a single response: precisely when no response is majority-preferred to all others, which holds with high probability in the same regime where scalar alignment is impossible [39]. The pole has its own published critique from inside alignment: where the equilibrium does collapse onto a single majority-preferred response, it forfeits the output diversity that repeated sampling exploits, with a proved k/(k+1) ceiling on the multi-sample win rate [67].

A parallel line does the same for *estimation*. A combinatorial Hodge decomposition splits the latent match-up structure of pairwise comparison data orthogonally into a gradient flow (the transitive, scalar-representable part) and a curl flow (the irreducibly cyclic part), with a shrinkage prior adapting between regimes without prespecifying which holds, and decision summaries built to remain well defined under intransitivity — including a dominance graph that need not be acyclic [49]. Latent-intransitivity extensions of Bradley–Terry fit sports data out of sample better than Bradley–Terry itself [50]. Where there are no alignment stakes, the reflex is to model the cycles rather than delete them. Cost is not zero on this side either: two equilibrium baselines were excluded from a set of language-model-scale experiments as computationally prohibitive, at roughly 10³ GPU-hours per configuration [44].

### D. What the two poles actually disagree about

The apparent enforce-versus-accept dispute concerns the object of the operator, not whether repair destroys information. The Nash family's argument is an *existence* argument: under genuine intransitivity the target of the standard pipeline does not exist, so a different solution concept is required. It is not, and has never been, an argument that projecting a single agent's realised choice sequence onto a consistent set is harmful. That holds of the Nash and maximal-lottery family by name, and should not be extended to the acceptance pole as a whole, which does contain harm-grounds objections to single-agent repair: a bounded agent that insists on complying with the coherence axioms must give up even approximate optimality on its own objective [37], and the exact loss decomposition prices scalar representability in units of representational fidelity [42]. Those objections are about optimality under compute limits and about learned preference *models*, not about one agent's own choice record. On that object the positions are compatible: one can compute a von Neumann winner over an intransitive population preference model and, separately, repair one agent's own cyclic judgments.

## 5. The ML/Alignment Front Applied to Agent Choice: Enforce or Accept?

Both camps have shipped working systems on real agents. The enforcement camp is larger and better evidenced on non-preference metrics; the acceptance camp has the stronger theory and the weaker identification. This front has a measurement-only wave too, the same contrast class §3A draws on the economics side:

- per-instance transitivity diagnostics with conformal prediction sets [56];
- behavioural consistency used as an uncertainty signal, with abstention rather than repair as the response [17];
- elicited utility structure that is coherent and motivationally inert [58];
- the measurement half of the non-transitivity judge audit [51];
- and the dominant reward benchmark, which scores accuracy on prompt–chosen–rejected trios with no consistency construct at all [40].

None of these changes anything either.

### A. The enforcers

**Inference-time judge repair.** Two inconsistency types are formalised: *score–comparison inconsistency*, where a response with a lower pointwise score wins the direct pairwise comparison, and *pairwise transitivity inconsistency*, covering circular chains and violations of transitivity of indifference. The repair has two parts — distribution-sensitive scoring, replacing a discrete integer rating with a continuous expectation over the judge's own probability distribution across rating tokens, and likelihood-aware aggregation, resolving detected ties and cycles by perplexity or token-level confidence — with no retraining and no human annotation [2]. With Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct as judge, the conflict ratio falls from 23.32% to 14.89% and the size-five non-transitivity ratio from 15.22% to 4.40%; across eight task categories and three judges **the non-transitivity ratio collapses from 18.74% to 4.40% on average**, reaching 0.00% in one cell and 32.85% → 4.46% in the hardest. In ablation, likelihood-aware aggregation alone drives inconsistency to 1.94% on Llama-3.1-70B and 2.83% on GPT-4o, from a 20.26% baseline on Llama-3.1-8B [2].

**Training-time score repair by isotonic projection.** Pairwise labels are read as a target partial order; raw pointwise scores are reindexed along a linear extension of that order and projected onto the monotone cone by the Pool-Adjacent-Violators Algorithm, then rounded back to the integer scale [3]. This drives training-corpus score-pair reversal conflicts to exactly 0% — a definitional consequence of the projection, and proved; the 20.15% pre-repair rate and the 20.46% that inference-time probabilistic aggregation is said to retain on the same corpus appear only in the source's abstract and are substantiated nowhere in its twenty-three pages, seven tables or six appendices [3]. It does carry a genuine quality theorem: if the partial order induced by the pairwise labels is consistent with ground-truth scores, the isotonic regression is weakly closer to ground truth in L2, strictly closer if any comparable pair was violated [3]. That is the strongest theoretical result in favour of enforcement anywhere in the corpus — and its two preconditions are the whole story.

The source's own remark concedes that the round-half-up back to the integer scale is not a metric projection, and bounds the extra error at up to 0.87 on a one-to-five scale, so the theorem holds for an object that is not the deployed training label; every projection in the paper is over at most three numbers. And the operator requires the pairwise labels to induce a *valid partial order*; cycles among those labels are resolved not by the algorithm but by human expert re-annotation upstream, affecting fewer than 1% of comparison groups [3]. The published projection with a proof does not resolve intransitivity. It presupposes its absence.

**Dual-track relational enforcement.** Transitivity, symmetry/inverse and functional-uniqueness constraints are formalised over a relational output space and enforced twice. **At inference time:** constraint-aware prompting, verification enumerating the specific violated triples, targeted reflection on those triples, then transitive-closure propagation and deduplication. **At training time:** distilling teacher traces into Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen3-8B students and aligning with Group Relative Policy Optimization — a policy-gradient method that scores a group of sampled responses against their own mean rather than against a learned value model — on a joint accuracy-and-consistency reward [4]. This is the only system running both timings under one consistency formulation, and its metric — micro-F1 against gold human annotations on a document-level relation-extraction benchmark — is fully exogenous to any preference judgment.

**A dedicated probabilistic and ordinal repair layer.** For probabilistic coherence it either queries a full distribution over a partition and normalises, or, when atomic and disjunctive queries are jointly inconsistent, solves a quadratic program returning the coherent distribution minimising weighted squared error against the model's raw statements subject to non-negativity and sum-to-one. For transitivity it builds a weighted pairwise-majority graph from token-probability differences and applies iterative max-di-cut, with variants proved to reproduce a Kemeny ranking exactly [1]. The layer is honest about the naive alternative: linear pooling of token-level probabilities did not improve coherence and sometimes made it worse than the raw output [1]. Its evaluation, however, verifies only mechanical restoration — that the repaired probabilities sum to one, and distance-to-Kemeny on synthetic election distributions. No downstream quality test is run.

**Judge-ranking repair with a length control.** Round-robin evaluation with Bradley–Terry aggregation, applied to reduce non-transitivity in automated judging, raises Spearman correlation with human arena rankings from 95.0% to 96.4% and Kendall from 82.1% to 86.3%; in a separate 20-model comparison the gains are 4 and 5.2 points, and — the detail that matters — with length-controlled debiasing applied to both methods the repaired ranking still improves by 1.4 Spearman and 4.2 Kendall points [51]. Separately, an inference-time rank-fusion method repairing both order and transitive inconsistency raises NDCG@10 — normalised discounted cumulative gain, scored against human relevance labels — from 65.38 to 71.51 on a standard retrieval benchmark [52]. A third operator in the same corner perturbs each prompt into variants, uses the resulting comparison graphs to identify and filter structurally inconsistent comparison patterns, and only then aggregates — graph-level consistency imposed on the data before ranking rather than after [70].

**Training-data purification by cycle deletion.** An evaluator model's pairwise preference labels are read as a tournament graph; strongly-connected-component analysis locates the pairs that induce non-transitivity, a normalised directed-graph structural entropy tracks overall preference clarity, and exactly those pairs are deleted before fine-tuning. The filtered evaluator shows a 13.8% reduction in non-transitivity and a 0.088 drop in structural entropy, and the discarded pairs validate as genuinely noisy rather than merely inconvenient: 34.4% inter-annotator agreement against 52.6% for the retained ones, and 51.2% model–human consistency against 80.6% [64].

**Belief-drift stabilisation at both timings.** A seed-answer prompting strategy at inference and a self-consistency loss that amortises early-stage drift into the weights at training both target martingale coherence — the requirement that a belief be a fair forecast of its own later revisions. Both substantially reduce drift and raise predictive coherence on multiple-choice question answering *without* changing accuracy [65]. That is the corpus's one clean null on an exogenous metric, and it is a distinct third direction from the positives and the failures below.

**Objective-level axiom enforcement.** Where each pairwise comparison is labelled by more than one annotator, replacing per-annotator examples with a single majority-vote binary indicator causes the maximum-likelihood estimate to implement the **Copeland rule** — rank by number of pairwise majorities won — rather than Borda, and the resulting reward model provably satisfies Pareto optimality, majority consistency, pairwise-majority consistency and Condorcet consistency under fully general cyclic profiles [8]. A companion result explains why standard practice has not visibly suffered: under one annotator per comparison, standard reward modelling already satisfies pairwise-majority and Condorcet consistency [8]. The stated limitation is honest — the modification needs substantially more labellers and, under current datasets, reduces to standard practice.

### B. The accepters, and what their numbers actually show

The acceptance camp's systems are real and their results are good. The specific inference that enforcing an order costs quality does not survive contact with the tables.

**The capacity confound is structural, not sloppy.** A general preference model embeds responses into a latent space and reports outperforming a Bradley–Terry reward model on a standard reward benchmark, with downstream gains after post-training [12]. Its successor decomposes preferences into orthogonal transitive and cyclic components, criticising the earlier model for entangling hierarchy with cyclicity [13]. Both state that Bradley–Terry is the dimension-one special case of their model. The cycle-tolerant arm is therefore a **strict superset model class**, and varying the coherence assumption inside a preference model *necessarily* varies its expressive capacity — exactly what the exact loss decomposition of §4B says must happen [42]. These comparisons cannot separate coherence from capacity, by construction — and when the scalar baseline is independently retrained rather than quoted, it scores 79.76 against the 74.85 originally reported, so roughly five sixths of the cycle-tolerant arm's headline advantage is baseline weakness [13].

**The headline numbers are also widely mis-transmitted.** The frequently quoted +8.31-point win-rate margin is a raw, length-uncorrected figure drawn from the single most length-inflated cell, in which the treated arm emitted 65% more tokens; on the length-controlled version of the same metric in that cell the difference is −0.12, and the cycle-tolerant arm loses in 18 of 24 head-to-head cells and in all eight final-iteration cells [12]. The better-controlled replication, with all arms retrained from scratch under a single objective, finds a wash: nine wins to eight with one tie across eighteen downstream cells [13]. And the +1.23% reward-benchmark margin often cited alongside is a margin of one cycle-tolerant decomposition over another cycle-tolerant embedding model — not a margin of relaxing coherence over enforcing it [13]. No exogenous metric appears in either of these two papers; every downstream number in them is an automated-judge win rate or a preference-agreement score — a claim about these two general-preference-model papers, not about the acceptance camp at large, since the self-play descendants of the Nash objective do report standard capability-leaderboard numbers.

**One dose–response curve is published, and it favours transitivity.** The same replication's appendix carries the only graded coherence treatment in this literature. Nine levels of λ, the early-training weight on the transitive component relative to the cyclic one, are traced against length-controlled win rate: an inverted U with an interior optimum at λ = +1.0 (41.90), a monotone decline into the cyclic-first regime (40.21 → 38.66 → 37.27), a further decline at the extreme transitive tilt (40.15 at λ = +2.0), a span of 4.63 points, and response length flat across the sweep, so not a verbosity artefact [13]. The authors' own reading is that λ > 0 generally performs better while λ < 0 consistently underperforms. The curve is single-run with no seeds or intervals, on one benchmark at one iteration index; its endpoints — pure scalar, pure cyclic — are off the curve; and λ is a schedule tilt on a component of a learned preference proxy, not a quantity of incoherence removed. Within those limits it is a graded coherence treatment scored on downstream generation quality, and its direction is pro-transitivity — counter-evidence to the acceptance camp from inside the acceptance camp's own paper.

**The multi-objective case has the same shape.** A Blackwell-approachability solution concept, well defined under multi-objective intransitivity, beats a checklist-scalarization baseline in pairwise judge win rate roughly two-thirds of the time, with no significant capability regression [53]. But its own *scalarizing* ablation — the arm that collapses a rubric into one aggregate judge score, i.e. imposes a single order — improves on the base policy in three of the four headline cells and wins the length-controlled instruction-following cell outright over the full non-scalarized method [53]. Meanwhile the scalarization baseline it is compared against was itself reported, in its own paper, as the only alignment method to improve performance on every one of five benchmarks [54]. And in the original Nash-learning experiments, evaluated by a held-out preference model, the Bradley–Terry pipeline is the *strongest single arm against the supervised baseline*, winning 99% of comparisons; only a narrow band of mirror-descent mixing coefficients beats it head to head, and the worst arm is attributed by the authors themselves to preference hacking [43].

The conclusion is not that enforcement helps. It is that the sign of the *relaxation* effect is **unidentified**, for the two reasons just given. That is a stronger position than a claimed negative, since it cannot be overturned by a better-powered version of the same design.

### C. Where both camps agree: scalarisation manufactures cycles

Both sides accept that some observed intransitivity is produced by the aggregation rather than by the agent. A judge that reads a full checklist and emits one aggregate score is more intransitive, and more often lacks a Condorcet winner, than a judge that scores each item independently, at every batch size tested [53]. Position bias is a documented mechanism: ambiguous instructions show significantly higher non-transitivity, and with GPT-3.5-Turbo as judge over 96% of instructions are classified as ambiguous [51]. A rational-inattention account gives the cyclic residue a measurement: each pairwise label passes through a low-capacity evaluation channel, a scalar reward can represent observed log-odds only if their circulation around every cycle vanishes, and on a public arena dataset of 32 models, 74 pairs and 15,001 decisive votes the observed cyclic energy exceeds a parametric-bootstrap sampling-noise null at p = 0.008 [55]. The same work exhibits a three-item construction in which every individual pairwise majority points in the deliberatively correct direction yet the scalar fit reverses the top two items, and states that the reversal is not a knife-edge feature [55]. Per-instance diagnostics confirm the practical scale: aggregate violation rates of 0.8–4.1% conceal that 33–67% of documents contain a directed three-cycle [56].

The implication for repair is direct. **If a cycle was manufactured by scalarising several objectives, projecting it onto a single acyclic order is itself a scalarisation** — the operator is downstream of its own cause and re-derives the losing baseline. If a cycle is noise within a single objective, repair is exactly the right operation. Cycle source is therefore an empirical question about a given agent's data, not a matter to settle by assertion, and the instrument for settling it — the orthogonal gradient/curl decomposition — already exists and has never been pointed at an agent's own choices [49].

### B. Reading the table: two columns are nearly empty

*Formally minimal.* Only three entries perform a genuine minimum-distance projection, and each is minimal in a space that is not choice space: probability space [1], the score vector of a reward head under an ordering supplied as input [3], and the monotone-calibration cone [9]. Minimal perturbation of an agent's own chosen bundles appears nowhere.

*Exogenous metric.* Five entries score against something no preference judgment enters — gold-annotated extraction F1 twice [4], human relevance labels [52], realised human consumption [5], multiple-choice QA accuracy [65] — one further exogenous reading is negative [10], and the rank-fusion gain is attributable to positional-bias correction rather than to transitivity (§7A). Everything else is graded by a preference judgment, by the axiom itself, or not at all.

*The diagonal.* Every entry targeting GARP scores an exogenous outcome or nothing at all; every entry scoring a preference-judged outcome targets transitivity or monotonicity instead. No cell holds both a revealed-preference axiom over budget sets and a downstream competence measure. The budget-set qualifier is doing work there and should be defended rather than assumed. Under the abstract definition — rationalizability of a choice function over menus — a judge choosing between two responses *is* an agent choosing from a two-element menu, entries 1, 2, 3 and 12 do repair an agent's own choice function, and rationalizability has been tested price-free as acyclicity of a directed graph. What the budget-set instantiation adds is three things a tournament over responses does not define: a cardinal, money-metric dose; an outcome denominated in the same units as the perturbation; and a menu the experimenter sets rather than one the judge is handed. The unoccupied cell is that conjunction, not the bare phrase "an agent's own choices". SARP is enforced by nobody; WARP is enforced only obliquely, through an independence penalty in a fine-tuning loss [6]. The census does not merely list occupants; it exhibits the shape of the hole.

## 7. Does Enforced Coherence Change Downstream Decision Quality? The Empirical Record

The question names two outcome classes and they are populated very differently. **Decision quality** — the agent's own choices graded against a payoff — has been tested in both directions, and the results are not a wash: they obey a rule, but a rule that leaves an adverse prior standing. **General task performance** — whether enforcement carries a capability tax — is near-untested: one paper reports no significant capability regression [53], one no measurable training tax [57], one accuracy preserved under a coherence loss [65], and the single work that installs axiom terms directly in weights ran no capability suite at all [6].

### A. The positives, ranked by exogeneity of the outcome

Two results are scored on outcomes into which no preference judgment enters. Constraint-targeted verification raises document-level relation-extraction F1 from 0.351 to 0.468 against gold human annotations, **a gain of 0.117**, while three established generic self-reflection baselines run under identical conditions all *lose* ground, to 0.273, 0.271 and 0.290 [4]. The diagnosis is that unanchored self-criticism acts as a noise channel: the baselines emit 11.3, 10.7 and 7.9 triples on average against 16.0 in the gold standard, systematically deleting correct predictions they cannot re-verify. The constraint ablation localises the gain — disabling transitivity enforcement alone costs 0.113 F1, about 22%, and collapses the transitivity consistency score from 0.961 to 0.093 [4]. On the training track the same formulation raises consistency from 0.643 to 0.727, above the raw teacher's 0.707, while F1 rises from 0.473 to 0.492 simultaneously, and cuts raw transitivity-violation counts by 26.5% with the number of transitivity-relevant triples nearly unchanged — evidence that the repair is structural rather than suppression [4]. Second, GARP-consistent generative enforcement cuts squared bundle-prediction error against realised human consumption, on the panel described in §3B [5].

Gold-label and human-judged outcomes supply four more. Judge repair raises pairwise exact-match agreement with human-verified gold labels by 1.19 to 6.85 points while cutting inconsistency, and preference data selected by the repaired judge lifts downstream length-controlled win rates on two 7–8B policies [2]. Round-robin repair improves rank correlation with human rankings and survives the length control [51]. Rank fusion raises NDCG@10 by more than six points against human relevance labels [52] — but that gain decomposes badly for the coherence thesis. In-context ordering and calibration, which attack positional bias, carry essentially all of it, while the multi-ranker aggregation that actually targets transitivity moves the headline cell by +0.01 and another by −1.12; individual components lose up to 2.49 points; transitivity is never re-measured after the repair, the paper's triad counts being computed on unrepaired comparisons; and on the second benchmark the repaired pairwise ranker (65.10) does not beat the listwise baseline (65.49) [52].

Isotonic score editing yields a reward model whose overall benchmark score reaches 77.96, within 0.13 points of a proprietary teacher; its 10.13-point margin over the strongest open baseline is against a different training recipe, and the matched comparison — raw distillation on the same four-paradigm corpus — is a gain of 0.87 that also requires the subsequent reinforcement stage (§7D). Its signal wins roughly 69% of 68 head-to-head comparisons judged by three human annotators, on generated video rather than closed-loop physical execution, with no confidence interval and no significance test, as the paper's own limitations state [3].

### B. The three failures, across three axiom systems

Allowing 97 returning human participants to revise a random subset of ten of forty prior budget-set choices moved mean Afriat efficiency *down* by 0.025 at first measurement and the Houtman–Maks index down by 0.262, and dropped CCEI test–retest reliability from 0.522 to 0.443 [11]. The Houtman–Maks figure needs its unit to be read correctly: that index is a count of jointly rationalizable trials out of forty, so −0.262 is a quarter of one trial — not a backfire but a nothing, and the efficiency delta beside it is the only one carrying weight. Nor does any of the four deltas carry a p-value, a confidence interval or a test statistic; this is a descriptive null on 97 participants, not a powered equivalence test.

Monotone recalibration of elicited belief probabilities left the conditional-independence violation substantially greater than zero in all sixteen model-by-domain cells and *increased* its estimated magnitude in fifteen, fourteen of them by margins outside the bootstrap intervals — from 0.0753 to 0.3295 in the worst case, a factor of 4.4 [9]. And an axiom-constrained latent model that forces one latent variable to behave as a log-odds, so that an event and its complement decode to probabilities summing to one by construction, improved held-out incoherence from 0.1366 to 0.0383 and held-out correlation from 0.7286 to 0.7328 — while held-out mean squared error *worsened* from 0.0927 to 0.1014 [10].

Three axiom systems, three research communities, three adverse results. Each is individually explicable, and the explanations are the point.

### C. The rule: target alignment between operator and metric

The discriminating variable is target alignment between the operator and the metric it is graded on, not the direction of the coherence intervention. **That claim is this report's own; no source in either literature states it.** Every published gain projects onto the set its metric defines or a superset of it — transitive closure on the relation set that F1 grades, the GARP-consistent region on the bundle space that squared error grades, the monotone cone on the score vector that the reward benchmark grades, the cycle-free tournament on the judgments that exact match grades. Neither failure does. Monotone recalibration projects onto the calibration cone and is scored on conditional independence; the authors' own conclusion is that the residual dependence is structural, a mismatch between the elicited belief and the model's internal decision-relevant representation, not a calibration artefact [9]. The revision arm showed participants a *random* subset of choices, never identified the violating ones, supplied no consistency objective, and offered no guarantee that the revised set would contain fewer violations [11] — an invitation to reconsider, not a projection. Its own authors note that a prior study found revision *does* raise consistency, and concede in their own words that "due to the ineffectiveness of the intervention, we cannot rule out that a more effective intervention could increase the reliability of rationality measurements" [11].

The strict form of the rule — repair helps exactly when operator and metric share a target set — is too narrow, and two gains refute it. Judge repair targets transitivity and is graded on exact match against human labels; different sets, and it helps [2]. Relational enforcement targets three relational axioms and is graded on F1 against a human-annotated corpus; different sets, and it helps by a wide margin [4].

The general form that survives all eight outcome readings has two clauses. First, **repair helps when the enforced axiom is true of the process that generated the outcome measure.** Transitivity of a containment relation is a fact about the knowledge graph the annotations describe, so enforcing it adds correct structure. GARP is true of the utility-maximising process the theory posits, and Afriat's equivalence guarantees that sampling on GARP samples that process, which is why a GARP-consistent prior helps even for the 87% of real consumers who violate GARP themselves [5]. Monotone calibration does not imply belief-sufficiency; enforcing it adds nothing true and costs fit [9]. Second, **the sign of the operator's information flow matters.** Replacing a discrete integer rating with a continuous expectation over the judge's full output distribution *recovers* information that the coarse scale destroyed, and coherence improves as a byproduct [2]; a projection, by construction, removes information. The prediction is falsifiable: an information-adding coherence operator should improve coherence and accuracy together, an information-removing one should trade them off.

Three concessions travel with the rule. First, maximal target alignment is not sufficient, and the census holds the counter-case: the axiom-term fine-tune's operator is a sum of axiom penalties and its metric *is* axiom compliance, yet on the risk-averse target independence lands at 0.800 and transitivity at 0.9583, both below baseline, and independence falls 0.948 → 0.857 → 0.496 across the logit-scale sweep [6]. When the graded object is a *vector* of axioms rather than a single set, alignment on one component does not protect the others; the rule holds for a scalar metric defined by the projection target and fails for a multi-axiom one. Second, it explains the failures; it does not erase them. Three independent negatives across three axiom systems establish a real adverse prior that any new operator inherits. Third, the converse has never been tested directly: no study deliberately varies target alignment while holding operator and agent fixed. With eight outcome readings and two disjunctively available clauses, this is a hypothesis generated from the census rather than tested on it. What would falsify it is specific: an information-removing operator enforcing an axiom that *is* true of the outcome's generating process, which nonetheless degrades an exogenous metric.

### D. The dissociations inside the successes

Coherence is not a single scalar one simply increases, and three dissociations sit unremarked inside the strongest pro-repair result. Holding the reinforcement stage out of both arms, so that the only difference is whether the pointwise scores were isotonically cleaned, raw distillation reads 77.09 overall at 64.81 consistency while the cleaned arm reads 76.59 at 67.08: **the projection alone costs 0.50 points of quality and buys 2.27 points of consistency**, and the headline gain comes from the subsequent reinforcement stage, which adds 1.37 points of quality and 4.8 of consistency [3]. In the same paper, the best-scoring configuration overall (78.57, obtained by adding inference-time aggregation) has *worse* score-pair consistency (68.81) than the configuration it beats (77.96 at 71.90) [3]. And in its routing ablation, cleaning the pairwise side as well as the pointwise side is strictly worse (75.48) than cleaning the pointwise side alone, because the uncleaned pairwise pool's diversity outweighs its residual cycles [3]. Add the axiom-term fine-tune's purchase of independence at the cost of transitivity and continuity [6], and the pattern is clear: enforcing one axiom can consume another, and enforcing more of the same axiom can pass a point of negative return.

### E. Two dissociation studies, and the correlational tier

Two results attack the coherence–competence link from the other side. Agents trained via a discounted-reward stochastic-choice objective to *violate* completeness deliberately — no preference between trajectories of different lengths — and then scored on an independent task-usefulness metric scored 11% and 18% *higher* across five seeds under two policy-gradient algorithms, with train–test gaps 49% and 35% smaller and no measurable training tax. Two 8B-class open-weight instruction models fine-tuned to the same incomplete-preference property reached near-maximum usefulness across 40 held-out task families [57]. The caveats are real — usefulness is conditional on trajectory length by construction, the axiom is completeness rather than transitivity, and the manipulation is training-time — but this is the only clean, seeded, multi-arm test of the relationship in the corpus.

Separately, a study that reproduces coherent, model-specific utility structure under pairwise elicitation, and verifies that output quality *is* modulable by direct exhortation, reports that offering models outcomes they rate as highly preferred does not raise output quality relative to dispreferred outcomes or to none at all [58]. The elicited coherence is real and motivationally inert. A third dissociation comes from the same task family as the report's rank-fusion positive: treating rankings produced under equivalent candidate permutations as observations of an induced preference system, pairwise preference instability, global preference inconsistency and listwise output consistency track each other closely but *diverge* from recommendation effectiveness, and improving relevance or flattening position exposure does not restore stable pairwise preferences [69].

The correlational tier is informative and is not evidence for causation. Across 8,000 runs of four models on 200 multi-hop questions, tasks producing at most two distinct action paths reach 82–87% accuracy while those producing four or more reach 41–65%, a gap surviving difficulty controls, with failure-detection AUROC of 0.62–0.78 [17]. That is a useful uncertainty signal, and the intervention built on it is abstention. The correlation is also exactly what one expects if consistency and accuracy are both downstream of task difficulty and model competence.

The human-cognition literature supplies the cautionary parallel: a high-powered poker-judgment experiment finds a genuine positive coherence–accuracy correlation, 25 of 30 unconfounded correlations significantly positive with an expert-group coefficient of 0.24 (95% CI 0.14–0.33) — but attributes it to *expertise*, its sampling model generating both coherence and accuracy from one latent quantity, the number of mental samples drawn, and its power analysis showing that an influential prior null had only a 21% chance of detecting the true correlation [59]. The mechanism is a third variable, and underpowered designs manufactured the null. Models also systematically model *other* agents as more rational than they are, tracking expected-value maximisation with correlations of 0.93 and 0.94 for frontier systems against a human figure of 0.48 [60]; an agent both repaired toward coherence and used to simulate people will compound that bias rather than correct it.

### F. Can the instrument bear the inference? Partly, and not simply by scale

Any dose–response reading of these results depends on the coherence index being stable for a fixed agent, and the evidence on that splits by scale.

The human evidence is discouraging on its face. Across eight datasets and more than 1,600 participants spanning social, food, risk and ambiguity domains, not one of roughly forty intraclass correlation estimates for CCEI or the Houtman–Maks index reaches the conventional "good" threshold of 0.75; intermethod reliability under a change of presentation format alone runs 0.071 to 0.408 for CCEI, *worse* than five-month test–retest at 0.511 and 0.526 [11]. An instrument that agrees with itself more across five months than across a change of interface is not measuring a trait of the decision-maker. The authors rule out the charitable reading: within-subject coefficients of variation are low — median 15% for CCEI, 5% for Houtman–Maks, at twenty or more trials — so this is a genuine absence of between-subject variance rather than measurement error, and their prescribed remedy is a between-groups manipulation [11]. Two further details from the same source bear directly on any before/after repair design: the index *rises* on re-measurement, a practice effect (b = 0.037, p = 0.002), and an individual's measurement predicts that individual's next measurement roughly twice as badly as simply assuming the population mean [11].

The model-side evidence points the other way, and only for large models. Holding the model fixed and changing only the administration format, across 100 independent runs per model per domain, switching from multi-turn to single-turn elicitation drives Qwen2.5-7B's efficiency index from 0.980 to 0.739 in the risk domain and Llama-3.1-8B's from 0.953 to 0.841, both at p < 0.01, while GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V3 are essentially unmoved; open-ended versus multiple-choice response format reproduces the pattern; persona assignment has a sensitivity score of exactly zero and temperature between 0 and 1 moves none of the four [34].

**The reliability objection is scale-dependent in its format-sensitivity component, and only there.** Format alone moves a small model's index by up to a quarter of its range and leaves flagship indices unmoved — but format-invariance is not test–retest reliability, and treating the two as the same construct is what makes this look settled. The human failure is an intraclass-correlation failure driven by low *between*-subject variance, and a flagship pool whose indices all lie between 0.994 and 1.000 has almost no between-model variance: that instantiates the diagnosis rather than refuting it. No per-model test–retest ICC on fixed budget sets with fresh contexts has ever been measured, and the flagship format result rests on a single unreplicated study.

Nor does a ceiling index mean there is no headroom to *create* variation in coherence. The index compresses hard near 1: a mean of 0.997 is what a near-deterministic optimiser carrying three to five points of allocation noise on a hundred-point budget produces, and the human 0.963–0.985 band is what eight to twelve produces, so a factor of two to three in behavioural noise is squeezed into 0.03 index units. Headroom is also demonstrated rather than hypothesised — holding budget sets fixed and rewording only the prices drops mean CCEI to 0.698–0.901, and continuous-to-discrete framing to 0.780–0.908, with 32% to 88% of runs landing below 0.9 [34]. The lever needed to dose a frontier agent is presentational rather than absent, which is what §3A's framing sensitivity already implied. Repair is still best understood as a partial substitute for capacity — which is exactly why the largest measured repair gain, 6.85 points, lands on the smallest judge [2].

One wrinkle resists the simple story. Reasoning-tuned variants of a 14B-class model are *less* rationalizable than the non-reasoning version, making more trembling-hand errors [18], and specialised personas increase axiom violations and lower the efficiency index [33]. Capability along the dimension the field usually means is not monotonically coherence-improving; what predicts coherence is closer to how well the elicitation format matches the model's processing than to parameter count or reasoning effort [34].

### G. Why no existing estimate is identified

Every gain is graded on the operator's own target set, on a preference judgment, or on a gold standard whose generating process the enforced axiom happens to describe. Every failure occurs where operator and metric come apart — the only regime in which the question is meaningfully being asked. The one matched null-operator control anywhere in the corpus — the three generic self-reflection baselines of §7A, matched on the revision loop and roughly on compute, all three losing ground where the constraint-targeted version of the same loop gains 0.117 F1 [4] — is the strongest single piece of causal evidence in the literature, and it exists in exactly one paper. No published study varies choice-coherence on a fixed agent, holding capacity constant, with a graded rather than binary dose, and scores the result on a payoff no preference judgment enters. The decision-quality half of the question is populated and confounded; the task-performance half is close to empty.

## 8. Where the Two Literatures Miss Each Other

### A. The citation graph, measured

The gap between the fronts is not a gap in ideas; it is a citation failure. The label-free regularization proposal is, at freeze time, the only work citing the inference-time rationality layer, and it characterises that layer as post-processing of model outputs, categorically distinct from its own training-time penalties — accurate, but it does not extend the projection mechanism, performs no ordering search, and computes CCEI only as a scalar [7]. The isotonic score-editing paper has no forward citations at all, and describes its operator as a minimum-distance projection onto a monotone cone — vocabulary the revealed-preference literature has used since 1985 [21] — without citing that literature [3]. The central-bank study grounding agent consistency in Afriat is on no preprint server the machine-learning field routinely sweeps, and is invisible to an arXiv-only search [18]. The economics-front foundations — money pump [22], approximate-expected-utility distance [25], goodness-of-fit impossibility [16], NP-hardness of minimum-cost repair [23], integer-programming computation [15], optimal-transport recovery [26] — contain essentially no reference to language models. The bridges are few, recent, and built almost entirely from the economics side [7][5][18].

### B. What is being independently rediscovered

*Minimal projection onto a consistency-defined set.* Isotonic regression by pool-adjacent-violators [3] is a minimum-L2 projection onto an order cone; the non-linear least-squares index is a minimum-weighted-L2 projection onto the rationalizable set [16]. The same idea — with one difference the collision hides. The isotonic cone is **convex** and takes the ordering as an input; the GARP-consistent set is a **non-convex union over orderings**. What fails to transfer is the *guarantee*: projection onto a convex cone containing the truth is non-expansive in L2, which is why [3]'s "weakly closer to ground truth" theorem holds, and that property has no analogue over a union of polyhedra. The ordering search itself is not the open problem — the multiplier-free ordinal characterisation absorbs it into T(T−1) binaries inside a single mixed-integer program with no outer loop [15]. What is genuinely missing is the worked objective and its well-posedness, not the tractability.

*Cycle deletion.* Approximating a Kemeny ranking by max-di-cut [1] and computing the minimum cost index by deleting cheapest arcs [23] are both maximum-acyclic-subgraph problems. The economics side proved NP-hardness by exactly that reduction; the machine-learning side reaches for an approximation algorithm without citing it. Deleting the preference pairs that induce non-transitivity from an evaluator's training corpus [64] is Houtman–Maks by another name — maximal consistent subset by deletion — reached with no reference to the index, its complexity, or its two-goods threshold. The refusal is sometimes explicit and citable: the rank-fusion authors name Kemeny rank aggregation and decline it because such methods "are either NP-hard or not specifically designed for list aggregation" [52], which is a published statement that the minimal-perturbation route was seen and rejected on tractability grounds.

*Continuous severity measures.* Dutch-book magnitude, one minus CCEI and one minus a distance parameter are proposed as continuous penalties vanishing exactly when the axiom holds [7], while the alignment literature independently reports soft non-transitivity degrees, conflict ratios and non-transitivity rates with the same intent [2][51] — and neither has confronted the other's impossibility result, that a continuous index cannot be accurate on the general class [16].

*Measurement-error readings of violations.* The money-pump index doubles as a test of rationality under measurement error, which is why a panel in which four households in five violate GARP still cannot reject rationality [22]; the alignment literature reached the same posture independently, holding that a weak preference signal may reflect hidden evaluation difficulty rather than genuine indifference [55].

Vocabulary collisions deepen the invisibility. "Minimal perturbation" names an established subjective-expected-utility index on one side [25] and, informally, an isotonic projection on the other [3]. "Projection" means an L2 map onto a convex cone in one literature and a mixed-integer program over orderings in the other. "Consistency" means transitivity of pairwise judgments to one field and cycle-freeness of a revealed-preference relation over budget sets to the other. The scalar-reward existence theorem [39] and Afriat's equivalence [14] are in substance one result, proved from social choice on one side and from linear programming on the other, with neither citing the other's tradition.

### C. Each side holds the corrective the other needs

Economics has cardinal, graded violation measures that are continuous, bounded and zero exactly when the axiom holds [7], a mature complexity picture [15][23], and a proof of when the repair problem is ill-posed [16]. What it lacks is any agent-facing downstream metric: every application is to human consumption panels or survey respondents.

Machine learning has the downstream metrics, the scale, the training machinery, and one identification insight economics lacks entirely — that varying the coherence assumption inside a learned preference model necessarily varies its capacity [42], a structural confound with no analogue in the human-subject designs the indices were built for. What it lacks is a cardinal dose *in use*: its deployed operators and ablations count cycles and report non-transitivity rates [2][53] where the economics side has five graded indices and knows which are polynomial and which are NP-hard. The magnitudes do exist on this side — a cyclic energy tested against a parametric bootstrap null at p = 0.008 [55], the orthogonal curl component of a Hodge decomposition [49], and a seminorm measuring a pairwise utility's infimum distance from the additively separable set, which governs the convergence rate of a Markov-chain alignment procedure and its first-order agreement with the Nash objective [68] — but not one of them has ever been varied as an experimental dose. That is the sharper gap.

## 9. Verdict: What Is Occupied and What Remains Open

### A. Half one: occupied

Interventions that correct, project, repair or enforce revealed-preference consistency on an AI agent's choices or outputs exist at both timings, across seven axiom families, and at least two of those catalogued here carry formal guarantees: an isotonic projection proved to weakly reduce error against ground truth when its ordering precondition holds [3], and a reward-modelling modification proved to restore Condorcet, majority and pairwise-majority consistency and Pareto optimality under arbitrary cyclic profiles [8]. Minimal-perturbation projection onto a consistency-defined set exists in probability space [1] and in score space with a proof [3]. Adding the economics-front operators that exist but have never been aimed at an artificial agent roughly doubles the stock of repair machinery. Any claim that this cell is empty is unsustainable on the facts.

What is *not* occupied is narrower and precise. No published operator performs a minimal perturbation of an agent's own choice sequence over budget sets onto the GARP-consistent set. The one system that enforces GARP does so generatively, by rejection sampling before training, and its authors frame this as departing from the measurement-and-repair paradigm rather than instantiating it [5]. The one proposal that computes a GARP-based quantity uses it as a scalar penalty, searches over no orderings, and performs no projection [7].

### B. Half two: populated but unidentified

The coherence-to-quality relationship has been measured at least eight times across seven systems, with gains and reversals both real, and no reading meets all three requirements for an identified estimate — an unconfounded treatment, an exogenous outcome, a graded coherence dose (§7G). The three failures are not equally expensive to remedy. The capacity confound is structural wherever the treatment sits inside a learned preference model [42][12][13], and an exogenous outcome needs a benchmark nobody has built; but the graded-dose requirement is the cheap one, because cardinal indices already exist on both fronts — Afriat efficiency and the money-pump ratio on one [15], the transitivity seminorm, cyclic energy and Hodge curl on the other [68][55][49] — and have simply never been varied. The field has many data points and no clean estimate.

### C. Four open cells, and they are narrow

First, the continuous quantity-perturbation program — minimising the distance between an agent's realised bundles and the nearest GARP-satisfying bundles — is sketched in two sentences with no inequalities, no program and no complexity classification [15], and the one published attempt at it is a greedy, order-dependent heuristic carrying no optimality guarantee [62]; no complexity result exists for the corresponding least-squares index anywhere [16]. The neighbours bracket it without settling it: minimum-cost deletion is NP-hard [23], and the deletion index is polynomial at two goods and NP-complete from three [66]. The prior obstruction is well-posedness rather than tractability — the target set is not closed, so the minimum may not be attained and the distance may read zero on violating data [16].

Second, no coherence intervention on an artificial agent has been graded on a *cardinal* dose applied post hoc to a *fixed* agent — the only design holding capacity and training identical across levels of enforcement by construction.

Third, no study combines enforced coherence with an outcome into which no preference judgment enters *and* a distance-matched null-operator control that displaces the choices equally without improving consistency. Without that control, every positive is open to the objection that the displacement, not the coherence, did the work — and neither published reversal had one either. The human evidence sharpens the requirement: the index rises on simple re-measurement, so a before/after repair design without a distance-matched null arm cannot separate the operator from the practice effect [11].

Fourth, whether a given agent's cycles are multi-objective in origin or noise-driven is an empirical question nobody has answered on model-generated choice data, though the instrument exists [49] and the available evidence — transitivity indices of 0.957 to 1.000 [6], money-pump severity around 6% of expenditure in the human analogue [22] — points to the regime where minimal repair is cheap. That reading holds only as far as the indices do: a ceiling index is consistent with a coherent agent and with a test that could not have failed, and the two have not been separated for any agent whose index is quoted here.

One qualification belongs on the openness claims themselves. Full-text venue indexes — the computational-linguistics anthology, the philosophy preprint archive, the economics working-paper repositories, the social-science preprint server — resist automated retrieval, and four of six targeted venues could not be searched at all. The absence of a further occupant there is a gap in coverage, not a verified zero, and it is precisely where working-paper economics is published.

### D. Forward-looking assessment

Three developments look likely on a two-to-three-year horizon, and each follows from the census rather than from speculation about it.

**Convergence on cardinal dosing.** Binary cycle-counting is already the bottleneck in the machine-learning front's own ablations: a paper that can report reversal conflicts only as pre-repair or exactly zero cannot see the coherence–quality dissociations sitting inside its own tables at intermediate levels [3]. Once a graded index — Afriat efficiency, a money-pump ratio, a least-squares distance — is imported, the dose–response curves that the economics side computes in under two seconds [15] become available to alignment research. The obvious next experiment on the training side is equally cheap and equally unrun: an axiom penalty inserted directly into a fine-tuning loss [7] plus a standard capability suite, exactly the combination the one paper that installs axiom terms in weights declined to run [6].

**A split by cycle source and by scale.** If coherence headroom keeps shrinking with scale — flagship efficiency indices sit at 0.994 to 1.000 and are insensitive to administration format [34], though §7F's compression and rewording results leave that a genuine *if* — then choice repair becomes a technique for small, cheap and specialised deployments rather than for frontier systems, which is where the largest measured gains already land [2][4]. The multi-agent turn cuts the other way: cycles that are aggregation artefacts inside a single scalarising judge become *genuine* population facts once several agents with different objectives negotiate, and in that regime the impossibility results bind and equilibrium concepts, not repair, are the correct response [39][43][47]. The mistake to avoid is applying either where the other belongs.

**Exploitability becomes an attack surface rather than an illustration.** As agents transact on behalf of principals, the money pump stops being a thought experiment: a counterparty able to query an agent's preferences at scale can trade against the cycles it finds [1][36]. Current agents are conspicuously bad at exactly the decisions that would be priced — tool-using agents attain 3.9% to 24.0% micro strict success on budget-constrained escalation tasks, at most 7.3% economic consistency, and a threshold-crossing budget sweep changes one frontier model's escalation rate from 0% to only 3% [61]. The economics front has a dollar-denominated severity measure for that exposure [22][26], and it converts a coherence index into an underwritable quantity — a stronger reason to compute one than any safety argument currently on offer.

A closing caution that every source in this corpus supports: coherence is value-neutral. A perfectly rationalizable agent acts on *some* utility function, and nothing in Afriat's theorem, in de Finetti's, or in any money-pump argument constrains which one [7][36]. Enforcing coherence makes an agent legible and non-exploitable; it does not make it good. The strongest available reason to compute a coherence index is diagnostic — an agent whose choices cannot be rationalized at all cannot be audited by any tool either literature has built — and the strongest available reason to build the missing operator is that it is the one design that would say what enforcement actually costs.
