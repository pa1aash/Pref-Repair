# Adversarial audit of the exogenous-payoff design

Written 2026-08-26, before the dose-response result (`rho = 0.729`, `p < 0.0001`, 82% of repairs
improved payoff, per `docs/MAIN_EXPERIMENT_RESULTS.md` §4) is treated as reportable. This is the
hard gate required before Parts Two-Four of this session's mandate proceed. Method: read
`docs/MAIN_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL.md` §5's payoff definition and the actual scoring code
(`src/payoff.py`, `src/projection.py`, `src/budget_sets.py`, `src/analyse_main.py`), then test the
four adversarial questions empirically against `results/main_ccei.json` (142 kept traces, 85
GARP-violating with a computed projection) rather than only re-reading the prose justification.

**Verdict: CLEAN.** No re-analysis is required to fix anything; the checks below are additive
robustness evidence the paper's own robustness section should report, per the operator's framing
of this gate. One unrelated documentation discrepancy was found (§5 below) and is reported, not
silently patched.

---

## 1. Does the payoff share data with what determined the projection's direction?

**No shared derived quantity beyond the exogenous observables `(p, income)`.** `src/payoff.py`
imports only `numpy`; it has no dependency on `src/projection.py` or `src/ccei.py`. The optimal
bundle `x*_t = cd_optimal_bundle(p, incomes, alpha=0.5)` is a function of `p` and `incomes` alone
— `x` (the agent's raw choice) and `x̃` (the projected choice) never appear in its computation
(`payoff.py:12-18`). `p` and `incomes` are the budget line — generated by `src/budget_sets.py`
before any model was queried (`M, N ~ U[0.1,1]` i.i.d., symmetric between goods A and B, no
dependence on any agent's choices or on the 0.5/0.5 CD weights used later). Sharing the budget
line between the projection's constraints and the payoff's yardstick is expected and correct: both
are scoring the same choice problem. What would constitute a leak — the payoff's weights being
fit to, or the optimal bundle depending on, the agent's revealed choices — is not present in the
code.

**One near-miss, checked and ruled out.** `src/projection.py`'s `_cobb_douglas_warm_start` fits
Cobb-Douglas *shares* to the agent's own observed `(p, x, I)` — this looked, on first read, like it
could be a channel through which agent-specific information reaches the "exogenous" comparison
point. It is not: this share-fitted bundle is a different quantity from the payoff's fixed 0.5/0.5
`x*`, and — more importantly — it is never passed to the solver. `scipy.optimize.milp` has no
incumbent-injection hook, and the code does not attempt one; `x_cd` is used only to compute
`ceiling_dose`, which is logged in `solve_info` and never read by `analyse_main.py` (the reported
`dose_l1` comes from `res.x[n_vars:n_vars+n_d].sum()`, the MILP's own objective value). The
direction and magnitude of every reported projection is therefore driven solely by L1-distance
minimization subject to the GARP ordering constraints and budget exhaustion — nothing in that
objective references `U_exo` or the 0.5/0.5 weights.

## 2. Is the payoff's optimum independent of the agent's revealed preferences?

**Yes, confirmed both by code and empirically.** `cd_optimal_bundle` takes `(p, incomes, alpha)`
only — `alpha=0.5` fixed before any data collection, never re-estimated. This satisfies the
protocol's own three-part exogeneity argument (§5, properties i-iii), and reading the code confirms
the prose matches the implementation.

The sharper version of this question — *does GARP-consistency itself mechanically buy higher
payoff, independent of projection, in a way that would make "coherence" and "payoff" secretly the
same variable?* — is testable directly: compare raw (pre-repair) payoff between the 57 traces that
were already GARP-consistent (dose = 0, untouched) and the 85 that were not.

```
GARP-consistent traces   (n=57): mean raw_payoff = 0.7872
GARP-violating traces    (n=85): mean raw_payoff = 0.7899
Welch t-test: t = -0.070, p = 0.944
```

No difference. Whether a trace happens to already satisfy GARP has no detectable relationship with
its exogenous payoff. This rules out the concern that "more internally consistent" and "scores
higher on `U_exo`" are quietly the same thing at the population level — if they were, consistent
traces would show higher raw payoff even before any projection is computed, and they do not.

## 3. Hand-derived mechanism check on concrete traces

Five traces spanning the full dose range (dose = 0.17 to 111.64) were re-run through `project()`
and inspected directly: raw vs. repaired payoff, mean expenditure share on good A (raw vs.
repaired, vs. the CD-optimal 0.500), and the cosine similarity between the actual movement
`(x̃ − x)` and the direction toward the exogenous optimum `(x* − x)`.

| Trace | dose (L1) | raw payoff | repaired payoff | Δ | cos(movement, toward-optimum) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| llama3.2:3b, reciprocal, rep 19 | 0.17 | 0.8702 | 0.8703 | +0.0001 | 0.251 |
| qwen1.5b, baseline, rep 18 | 4.81 | 0.9535 | 0.9541 | +0.0006 | 0.160 |
| llama3.2:3b, baseline, rep 11 | 11.10 | 0.8674 | 0.8640 | **−0.0034** | **−0.354** |
| qwen1.5b, multiturn, rep 16 | 22.54 | 0.8464 | 0.8637 | +0.0173 | 0.241 |
| qwen1.5b, reciprocal, rep 12 | 111.64 | 0.3347 | 0.4086 | +0.0739 | 0.314 |

Two things this rules out: **(a)** the movement is not simply "the MILP aims at the CD optimum" —
cosine similarities are low (0.16-0.31) for the four improving traces, meaning most of the L1
distance the solver spends is orthogonal to the direction that would trivially buy payoff, spent
instead satisfying the GARP ordering constraints (1)-(4) that have nothing to do with `U_exo`.
**(b)** the one trace where payoff *worsened* after repair is exactly the one where the movement
direction was *negatively* aligned with the direction toward the optimum (cos = −0.354) — the sign
flips together with the mechanism it should flip together with if the relationship is real rather
than definitional. If the payoff gain were an artifact of the scoring function rewarding smaller
perturbations generically, dose and the sign of Δpayoff would not track this directional geometry;
they do.

## 4. The severity-confound check (does dose just proxy for "confused, generally bad" traces?)

This is the sharpest version of the adversarial question and the one most likely to have killed the
result. Tested directly on all 85 GARP-violating traces (`results/main_ccei.json`):

```
dose vs. raw_payoff (pre-repair):        Pearson r = -0.411, p = 9.4e-05   [confound IS present]
raw_payoff vs. delta_payoff:             Pearson r = -0.415, p = 8.0e-05   [ceiling effect IS present]
dose vs. delta_payoff (unconditional):   Pearson r =  0.821, p = 7.0e-22   [the headline number]
```

The confound is real: larger-dose traces do start from worse raw payoff, and worse-starting traces
do have more room to improve — exactly the mechanism the audit brief warned about (severity of
violation proxying for something else, here plausibly generation quality/effort). The question is
whether it *explains away* the dose-payoff relationship.

```
Partial correlation, dose vs. delta_payoff | controlling for raw_payoff:  r = 0.784, p = 7.3e-19
OLS: delta_payoff = 0.000577*dose - 0.00844*raw_payoff + 0.00524
     dose coefficient:       t = 11.43, p < 1e-16   (survives, dominant)
     raw_payoff coefficient: t = -1.36, p = 0.178    (not significant once dose is in the model)
```

It does not explain it away. Controlling for raw payoff attenuates the correlation only slightly
(0.821 → 0.784) and dose remains the dominant, highly significant predictor; raw payoff's own
independent contribution to the improvement is not statistically distinguishable from zero once
dose is included. Severity of violation predicts *how much room there is to improve* (the
ceiling-effect correlation above), but it does not predict *how much repair actually buys*, beyond
what dose already captures. This is the single most important robustness check in this audit and
should be reported explicitly in the paper (see below).

## 5. Unrelated finding: warm-start documentation does not match the implementation

Not a contamination issue, but surfaced during the code read and reported rather than silently
fixed, per standing practice (`docs/METHOD_NOTE_Q3.md`'s own convention). `METHOD_NOTE_Q3.md`
("Feasibility is free, and gives a warm start", line 494) and
`MAIN_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL.md` §4 ("feed the resulting rationalisable sequence as an incumbent")
both describe the Cobb-Douglas warm start as being fed to the solver as an incumbent. The actual
implementation (`src/projection.py:133-139`) computes it but never passes it to
`scipy.optimize.milp` — there is no incumbent-injection call, and the code's own comment says why
("scipy.milp has no incumbent hook, so this is used solely as a sanity ceiling on the reported
distance"). This does not affect any reported number (`dose_l1` is the solver's true objective
value, independently verified via `garp_holds` on every returned `x̃`; MIP gaps were ≤8.1e-5 per
`MAIN_EXPERIMENT_RESULTS.md` §0, so the solves were effectively exact regardless of warm-starting).
It is purely a spec/implementation mismatch in the prose. **Action for Part Four's method section:
describe what the code does** (`x_cd` computed as a sanity-ceiling diagnostic only, not as a solver
incumbent), not what the method note recommended. Flagged to the operator in this session's closing
report; not something this audit silently patches in `METHOD_NOTE_Q3.md` or the protocol.

## What the paper's robustness section should say

State explicitly, per the GATE's requirement:

1. The exogenous payoff's optimum `x*_t` is a closed-form function of `(p_t, I_t)` alone, verified
   by code inspection to never reference `x_t` or `x̃_t`; the projection's objective is L1 distance
   under GARP constraints alone, verified to never reference `U_exo` or its weights.
2. GARP-consistency status alone does not predict raw payoff (`t=-0.07, p=0.94`, `n=57` vs. `n=85`)
   — ruling out a population-level mechanical link between "coherent" and "high-payoff."
3. Dose correlates with pre-repair payoff (`r=-0.41`), so a naive reading of the headline
   correlation risks a ceiling-effect confound. **This is tested directly**: dose predicts
   Δpayoff even after controlling for raw payoff (partial `r=0.78`, OLS `t=11.4, p<1e-16`), while
   raw payoff's own marginal contribution is not significant. The dose-response relationship is not
   an artifact of "bad traces have more room to improve."
4. Directional geometry on hand-checked traces (movement vs. direction-to-optimum) is only weakly
   aligned on average and sign-consistent with the one trace where repair *hurt* payoff — evidence
   against the mechanism being a disguised optimization toward the payoff target.

**Gate resolution: proceed to Parts Two-Four. The headline result survives.**
